SEO vs AEO vs GEO: what's the difference, and do you need all three?

By Content Authority Lab · 22 September 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer: SEO gets your page ranked in search results. AEO gets your content chosen as the direct answer. GEO gets your brand cited or recommended inside AI-generated responses. They are not three separate strategies. They are three layers of the same goal: being the most useful, most trustworthy source on a topic.

What is SEO?

Search engine optimization is the practice of making a page easy for search engines to crawl, understand and rank. It covers keyword research, search intent, on-page elements (titles, meta descriptions, headings, internal links), technical health and authority signals like backlinks.

SEO is still the foundation. AI systems very often pull from pages that already rank well, so if Google can't find or trust your page, an AI assistant is unlikely to either.

What is AEO?

Answer engine optimization means structuring content so a search engine or voice assistant can lift a clear answer straight out of it. Think featured snippets, People Also Ask boxes and Google's AI Overviews.

In practice, AEO looks like:

  • Asking the question in a heading, then answering it in the first one or two sentences below.
  • Using numbered steps for processes and tables for comparisons.
  • Adding a short FAQ section that answers the follow-up questions real people ask.
  • Using structured data (schema markup) where it fits.

In one of my own projects, Google lifted a single subsection, step 6 of a hair-care guide, straight into the snippet for a #1 result. That only happens when each section can stand on its own.

What is GEO?

Generative engine optimization focuses on large language model tools like ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Copilot. These tools don't show ten blue links. They write an answer and sometimes cite sources. GEO is about making your content the kind of source they choose to quote.

What seems to help most:

  • Specific, verifiable facts: numbers, dates, named sources and first-hand testing.
  • Clear entities: consistently naming your brand, products, locations and the people behind the content.
  • Demonstrated expertise (EEAT): author bios, credentials and original insight, not a rewrite of the top three results.
  • Being mentioned elsewhere: reviews, directories, industry sites and social platforms all shape how AI tools describe you.

So, do you need all three?

Yes, but you don't need three separate content teams. A well-researched page with a clear structure, direct answers and real expertise serves all three at once. The order I work in is:

  1. Get the SEO basics right: the right topic, the right intent and a crawlable, well-linked page.
  2. Structure for answers: question-led headings, answer-first paragraphs, lists, tables and FAQs.
  3. Add what only you can add: first-hand data, local context, expert input and sources. That is what makes AI tools, and people, trust you.
Write for the reader first. Structure for the machine second. Content that does both will keep working however search changes.

Frequently asked questions

Is SEO dead because of AI?

No. AI answers still rely heavily on content that search engines have already crawled and ranked. SEO has become the entry ticket rather than the whole game.

How do I know if AI tools mention my brand?

Ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini the questions your customers ask, and note whether you are cited. Several SEO tools now also track AI Overview and AI assistant visibility.

Does this apply to Arabic content?

Absolutely, and there is often less competition. Well-structured, genuinely useful Arabic content is still scarce in many niches, which makes it a real opportunity.
